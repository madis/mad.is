# What socialists (and all collectivists) really want?

It is quite common in the political discourse but also in the opinions expressed by many people, that some people must be helped and can only be helped through the actions of the state. As the state doesn't produce anything (it is an imaginary entity), the only way to obtain resources to help somebody is by forcefully taking it by the producers via taxes (current or future) or inflation.

I'm sure that most of these good willing people would oppose to robbery. The reason why they so readily support state intervention in these cases is that the act of robbing is delegated, anonymized and separated from the use of these funds to such an extent that it becomes easy to ignore the source of funds. I have never seen a politician walk from door to door with a bat or a gun demand the monthly payment. But they do like present themselves in front of cameras when announcing the opening of a new hospital, stadium or road.
The preluding "fund raising" (act of robbery) is often shown in movies as something that a mafia does. But the underlying operation is of the same nature - either you pay "voluntarily" or we'll take what we deem necessary and if you try to protect yourself against this robbery you will either end up in jail or dead. 

When the government actions are shown for what they really are, often a cognitive dissonance takes place. People who seriously think they are voting for and supporting a good cause, a politician who promises to help others, will cite you the standard responses they've learned from government schools, television and the print media. Things like "But who will help the poor?", "If the government didn't do it, nobody would", "The capitalists only want money, the government helps people", "Taxes are not robbery, I would pay them anyways" or "I don't mind paying taxes" etc.

In this article I'm not going to address the excuses people give for why they support the state. It can briefly be summarized as "if everybody wanted to do exactly what the government is doing, they could already be doing it by themselves by donating money or offering the services themselves". Rather I will inquire into what are the deeper motivations that lead people to this intellectual short-sightedness and stubborn incoherency. In the rest of the article I will use the term "collectivist man" to designate these people.

The axiom of action defined by Ludwig von Mises, resumed briefly, states that human beings engage in purposeful behavior to achieve desired ends by employing scarce means. It is self-evident and irrefutable because any attempt to deny it (e.g., arguing against it) constitutes a purposeful act of persuasion, thereby performing the very action being denied. 

So the collectivist man also acts. He tries to reach his desired ends. So for the collectivist man who supports the state, he must think that something good for him will come out from it.  Either he gains something positive or gets rid of something negative by supporting the state or is faced with losing something positive or gaining something negative when he withdraws his support to the state.
